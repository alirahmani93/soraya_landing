# Gulf market reference

Public research, August 2026. Figures are indicative bands from retail and trade
sources, not quotes. They set expectations and sanity-check a price; they never
replace what an actual Omani buyer says.

## Oman retail saffron, per gram

| Grade | Typical retail band (OMR/g) |
|---|---|
| Super Negin | 3.5 – 5.5 (Muscat premium: 4 – 6) |
| Negin | 3.0 – 4.5 (Muscat premium: 3.5 – 5) |
| Sargol | 2.5 – 3.5 |
| Pushal | 1.8 – 2.8 |

Broad retail sits at OMR 1.5 – 6/g, with outliers to 17 for boutique presentation.
That spread *is* the opportunity: the gap between 3 and 17 is not saffron, it is
packaging, story, and where it sits on the shelf.

Oman grows no saffron. Everything is imported, mostly Iranian. This cuts both
ways — no domestic-producer sentiment to fight, but also no novelty in "Iranian
saffron" alone. Zanjan single-origin and the family farm are the differentiators,
not the country.

## What the Soraya box actually competes with

Not loose saffron. In the buyer's mind it sits beside premium dates, artisanal
chocolate, and oud gift sets — the corporate and hospitality gifting category.
That category prices on presentation and occasion, and tolerates a much higher
markup over commodity content than a spice shelf does.

Consequences for pricing:

- Anchoring the box against per-gram saffron math leaves money on the table.
- The drawer cube is not a cost to minimise. It is the reason the category
  applies at all. Cut it and the box falls back to competing on OMR/gram.
- Personalisation carries real weight in GCC gifting — a recipient's name or a
  tailored message is perceived as substantially more valuable than the same item
  unpersonalised. Cheap to offer, disproportionate effect on a corporate order.

## Seasonality

Ramadan and Eid al-Fitr dominate GCC gifting; corporate buyers plan both together.
Two implications:

- **Lead time works backwards from Ramadan.** Rigid boxes run 10–15 working days
  after artwork approval, plus freight, plus clearance. A corporate order placed
  in Ramadan is an order for next year.
- The off-season is for sampling and listing, not for pushing volume.

## Buyer behaviour worth remembering

- Physical gifts carry weight in B2B relationships here; this is a real channel,
  not a novelty.
- Quality expectations extend to the packaging itself, not only the contents.
  A premium product in ordinary packaging reads as a mid product.
- Halal and alcohol-free are assumed, not selling points. Nothing in the Soraya
  range raises a question, but the certification paperwork may still be asked for.

## The 100 mesghal minimum

Omani buyers quoted a 100 mesghal minimum order. That is ~460.8 g of saffron
(or 500 g if they meant the 5 g rounded mesghal — confirm which).

At 2 mesghal per Soraya box, 100 mesghal is 50 boxes. Rigid drawer boxes
typically MOQ at 100 units. **The packaging minimum is the binding constraint,
not the saffron minimum**, and it is roughly double. Any conversation about
first-order size should start there.
