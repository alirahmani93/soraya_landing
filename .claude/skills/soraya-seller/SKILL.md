# Selling Soraya

Two different sales happen here and they are not the same conversation.

**The trader sale** is about margin, reliability and shelf turn. A Muscat buyer
does not care that the saffron is beautiful; they care whether it moves and
whether you will still be answering the phone in six months.

**The end-customer sale** is about the moment the box opens. Nobody buys a
drawer cube for its saffron content per riyal.

The product is the same. The pitch is not. Know which one you are in.

## Read first

`product/catalog.json` (what is actually in the box), `product/brand.json`
(positioning, palette, contact), and `soraya-pricing/references/gulf-market.md`
(what the market pays and when it buys). Never state a price without the pricing
skill; never state a contents figure from memory.

## The story, in one paragraph

A family farm in Zanjan grows saffron. The same soil gives two other things —
kakuti, a mountain mint that Iranians have brewed for generations, and Damask
rose. The box puts all three together in the form they are actually used in,
in a package built to be handed to someone. Sold out of Oman, grown in Iran,
one origin for all three drawers.

That is the whole story and it needs no embellishment. Do not reach for "ancient
Persian traditions" or "liquid gold" — the Gulf market has heard every saffron
cliché and the family farm is the part nobody else can copy.

## What actually differentiates

Ranked by how hard they are for a competitor to answer:

1. **Single origin, three crops.** Not a curated assortment bought from three
   traders. One farm, one region. This is the defensible claim.
2. **The kakuti.** Nobody in the Gulf is selling Ziziphora as a premium product.
   Saffron gift boxes exist; this one has something in it the buyer has not seen.
3. **The presentation.** Drawer cube, opened one drawer at a time. In a category
   judged on presentation, this is the price justification.
4. **Grade.** Negin, ISO 3632 Category I target. Necessary but not
   differentiating — every serious seller claims it. Have the assay to back it.

## The trader conversation

What they need to hear, in order: what it is, what it costs them, what they make,
how fast it moves, and what happens when it does not.

Bring to a first meeting: a physical sample, the ISO 3632 assay, a cost sheet you
can defend line by line, and a clear answer on minimum order. Leave the sample.

**The minimum-order conversation.** The buyers quoted 100 mesghal. Do the
conversion before they do: that is ~50 Soraya boxes, and the rigid box MOQ is
around 100 units — so packaging, not saffron, sets the real first order. Bringing
this up yourself reads as command of your own supply chain. Being told it by the
buyer reads as the opposite. See the trader skill.

**Objections and the honest answers:**

| Objection | Answer |
|---|---|
| "Too expensive against loose saffron" | Correct, and it is not competing with loose saffron. It is on the shelf beside dates and chocolate gift boxes. Ask what those sell for. |
| "What is kakuti? Nobody asks for it" | Nobody asked for matcha either. It is the reason this box is not the fifth saffron box on the shelf. Offer to sample it alone. |
| "Iranian supply is unreliable" | Fair concern, do not brush it off. Answer with the family farm, the fact that it is one relationship rather than a broker chain, and a stated lead time you will meet. |
| "Cut the packaging and we'll take it cheaper" | That deletes the product. Offer a smaller SKU instead — that is exactly what `iran` and `peace` are for. |
| "Send stock, we'll pay when it sells" | Consignment is a financing decision, not a sales one. Escalate; do not agree in the room. |

## Channels, in the order worth chasing

1. **Corporate and hospitality gifting** — hotels, banks, family offices, and
   companies buying Ramadan and Eid gifts. Largest orders, longest lead times,
   most sensitive to presentation, and personalisation lands hard here.
2. **Specialty grocers and gift retail in Muscat** — slower, but it is the shelf
   presence everything else references.
3. **Direct, through the landing page** — lowest volume, highest margin, and the
   only channel that gives you the customer directly. Not a business on its own
   yet; it is the proof the other two look at.

## Timing

Ramadan and Eid dominate. Corporate buyers plan both together and buy months
ahead. Work backwards: production lead time, plus freight, plus clearance. An
order placed *during* Ramadan is an order for next year — so the outreach calendar
matters more than the pitch does.

## Copy

For landing-page and catalog copy, keep the register the Gulf gifting market
expects: specific, unhurried, no exclamation marks, no manufactured scarcity.
Arabic is the primary language and is not a translation of the English — write it
as its own copy. See `references/messaging.md` for tested lines and the
things not to say.

## Red flags

| Thought | Reality |
|---|---|
| "Quote them a price to keep momentum" | Use the pricing skill. An off-the-cuff price becomes the ceiling forever. |
| "Say it is the world's finest saffron" | Every seller says it. Say Zanjan, say the farm, show the assay. |
| "Translate the English copy to Arabic" | Arabic is the primary market language. Write it first-class or it reads as an import. |
| "Discount to close the first order" | Discount the *SKU*, not the price. That is what the smaller boxes are for. |
| "Promise a lead time to sound responsive" | Check production plus freight plus clearance first. A missed first delivery ends the relationship. |
