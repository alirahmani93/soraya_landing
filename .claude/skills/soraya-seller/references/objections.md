# Objections, at length

The short table lives in `SKILL.md`. These are the four that need more than a
sentence, because answering them badly loses the account.

## "It's too expensive"

Establish what they are comparing against before answering. Two different
objections hide behind the same words.

*Comparing to loose saffron per gram.* They have mentally priced the saffron
content and concluded the rest is markup. Concede the arithmetic immediately —
it is correct — then move the frame: this sits beside premium dates and chocolate
gift boxes, not beside a spice jar. Ask what those retail for on their shelf. The
number they give you is the real ceiling, and now they said it.

*Comparing to another saffron gift box.* Different problem, and a fair one. Answer
with what is in the other box that is not in yours, and what is in yours that is
not in theirs. The kakuti is the answer. If they still say expensive after that,
they mean their margin is too thin — which is a pricing conversation, not a
positioning one. Take it to the pricing skill.

## "Nobody knows what kakuti is"

True, and it is the point. But "they'll learn to love it" is not an answer a
trader can act on. Give them something concrete instead:

- Offer to sample kakuti on its own, brewed. It sells itself in a cup far better
  than on a page — it is close to mint and thyme, and it is pleasant immediately.
- Point out that it does not have to carry the sale. The saffron does. Kakuti is
  what makes the box unlike the four others on the shelf.
- Offer the `iran` SKU as the low-risk entry, with the Soraya box
  alongside it. A trader who can start narrow will start.

If they still refuse the herb, they are buying commodity saffron and you are
competing on price. That is a different business — decide whether you want it
before discounting toward it.

## "Iranian supply is unreliable"

Do not brush this off. It is the single most reasonable objection anyone will
raise, and a defensive answer confirms it.

What is genuinely true and worth saying: this is one farm and one family, not a
broker chain — so there is one relationship to manage rather than four, and the
harvest is known months ahead. The Omani company holds stock locally, which puts
a buffer between the border and their shelf.

What to offer rather than argue: a stated lead time you will meet, a first order
small enough that failure is survivable for them, and local stock. Reliability is
demonstrated, not asserted. The first on-time delivery answers this objection
permanently; nothing said in the meeting does.

## "Take out the packaging and we'll buy it cheaper"

This deletes the product. Without the drawer cube the box is loose saffron in a
carton and it competes on OMR per gram, where a family farm cannot win against
volume importers.

Do not argue the point — redirect to range. `iran`, `sofreh`
and `peace` exist precisely so there is something to say yes to at a lower
price without dismantling the flagship. Let the buyer choose a smaller box rather
than a cheaper version of the big one.

The one thing that must not happen: shipping the Soraya box contents in inferior
packaging to hit a price. It sets the reference price for the brand at the bottom
of the range, and every conversation afterwards starts from there.
