# Messaging

## Positioning line

**English:** One farm in Zanjan. Three harvests. One box.

**Arabic:** مزرعة واحدة في زنجان. ثلاثة محاصيل. علبة واحدة.

Short enough for a box lid, specific enough that a competitor cannot use it.
"Zanjan" does the work — it is a place, and places cannot be claimed by a brand
that does not have one.

## The name

Soraya / ثريا is the Pleiades. The word carries in Arabic and Persian both,
which is unusual and worth using: a cluster of small bright things, presented
together. In Arabic-language copy the association is immediate and needs no
explanation. In English it usually does — one clause, then move on.

Do not build the whole brand on the astronomy. It is a grace note, not the story.
The story is the farm.

## Register

The Gulf premium-gifting register is unhurried and concrete. What that means:

- Short declarative sentences. Facts with numbers in them.
- No exclamation marks. No "discover", "indulge", "unleash", "journey".
- No manufactured scarcity ("only 50 left"). It reads as cheap in this category.
- No saffron clichés — "red gold", "liquid gold", "worth more than gold". The
  buyer has read them on every competitor's page.
- Specificity substitutes for adjectives. "Negin threads, 2 mesghal" beats
  "the finest premium saffron".

## Arabic is not a translation

Arabic is the primary market language and the site's default locale. Write the
Arabic as its own copy, then write English alongside — not the reverse. Copy
translated out of English carries English sentence rhythm and Gulf readers hear
it immediately, which undoes the premium positioning that the whole box exists
to support.

Keep product names transliterated consistently: زعفران نجين (Negin saffron),
ككوتي (kakuti), الورد الدمشقي (Damask rose). Fix the spellings once in
`catalog.json` and never vary them.

## Outreach — first contact with a Gulf trader

Short. No attachments on the first message. The goal is a sample in their hands,
not a sale.

> Good morning —
>
> We grow saffron on a family farm in Zanjan, and we have started packaging it in
> Oman with two other crops from the same land: kakuti and Damask rose. Presented
> as a gift box with separate drawers.
>
> We have samples in Muscat. Would you like one sent over?
>
> — <name>, Soraya

What that does: origin in the first line, the differentiator in the second, an
ask that costs them nothing. No price, no PDF, no adjectives.

## Corporate gifting outreach

Different buyer, different lever. They are solving a Ramadan or Eid obligation
for a list of recipients, and they care about presentation and personalisation.

Lead with: the box, the option to carry the recipient's name or the company's
mark, the lead time, and the order deadline that follows from it. Personalisation
is disproportionately valued in this market and costs little — offer it early.

State the deadline as a date, not as "lead times apply". A named date creates the
decision; a vague one postpones it.

## Never say

- Any origin other than Iran. Origin is a customs declaration.
- A price that has not come from the pricing skill.
- A lead time that has not been checked against production + freight + clearance.
- "Organic", "certified", or any grade claim without the paperwork in hand.
- Health or medical claims for kakuti or rose. Traditional use may be described
  as traditional use; a therapeutic claim is a regulatory problem in every GCC
  market and turns a food import into something else entirely.
