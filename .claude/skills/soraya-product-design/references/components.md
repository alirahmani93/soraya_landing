# The three crops

## Saffron — *Crocus sativus*

Dried stigmas. Zanjan, Iran.

**Grades.** Iranian saffron is separated by how the stigma is trimmed:

| Grade | What it is |
|---|---|
| Sargol | Red stigma tips only, no yellow or white. Finely cut, strong aroma, everyday cooking. |
| Negin | Long unbroken threads including the upper stigma. Both intense colour and rich aroma. |
| Super Negin | The longest, thickest, most uniform Negin. |
| Pushal | Red stigma with a short length of yellow style attached. |
| Dasteh / Khameh | Whole bunches, style included. Traditional, lowest colour strength per gram. |

Soraya uses **Negin**. The reason is visual as much as culinary — Negin's long
unbroken threads are what a drawer of saffron should look like. Sargol has
comparable strength and photographs as fragments.

**ISO 3632** grades saffron by three compounds measured spectrophotometrically:
crocin (colour), picrocrocin (taste), safranal (aroma). Category I is the top
band, crocin above roughly 190; Category II runs ~150–190; Category III ~110–150.

Get the assay. It is the only quality claim in this business that is not just
assertion, it is the paperwork a serious buyer expects, and it doubles as an
import document.

**Handling.** Degrades with light, heat, humidity and air. Absorbs neighbouring
aromas readily — which is exactly why it cannot sit loose in a drawer next to
mint and rose. Airtight, opaque, sealed at origin.

## Kakuti — *Ziziphora* spp.

Persian mountain mint, family Lamiaceae. Self-seeding, grows wild across Iran's
mountains, gathered rather than intensively farmed.

Aromatically it sits between mint and thyme, and it is pleasant on first taste to
someone who has never had it — which matters, because that is every Gulf customer.
Traditionally taken as a digestive and for gastrointestinal complaints.

**Say "traditionally used as", never make a health claim.** A therapeutic claim
turns a food import into a regulated product in every GCC market.

Two forms in the range: powder (30 g drawer) and whole leaf (display, and the
tea-bag blend). Whole display-grade leaf is a *selected* grade — intact leaf goes
in the box, broken leaf goes to the grinder. That selection is where the 0.9×
costing rule comes from; see the pricing skill.

This is the range's genuine differentiator. Saffron gift boxes exist everywhere.
Nobody in the Gulf is selling Ziziphora as a premium product.

## Damask rose — *Rosa × damascena*

The national flower of Iran, cultivated for centuries; the major commercial
products are rose water, rose oil, and dried buds and petals. Kashan, Fars and
Kerman are the classic growing regions.

Two forms in the range, and they do different jobs:

- **Buds** (20 g) — closed whole buds. The visual hero of the box. Buds photograph
  far better than petals, which flatten and lose colour on camera.
- **Petals** (20 g) — loose, for infusion and garnish. This is the form that
  actually gets used.

Colour fades with light. Anything in a display drawer needs UV consideration or
it browns on a shelf, and a browned rose drawer undoes the whole presentation.

## What they have in common

All three are aromatic, all three degrade with light and heat, and all three will
happily take on each other's smell if they share air. The drawer is the
presentation layer; the seal is a separate layer inside it. Any design that
treats the drawer as the container has already failed.
