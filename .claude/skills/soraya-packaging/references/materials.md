# Materials and inner containers

## Where money shows

Ranked by visible effect per riyal spent. Spend from the top.

1. **Interior lining.** A printed or fabric-lined interior against a raw grey
   board interior is the single largest perceived-quality difference, and it is
   the item most often cut from a quote to hit a price. Do not cut it.
2. **Outer wrap texture.** Uncoated or textured stock reads premium. Gloss
   lamination reads mass-market no matter what it cost.
3. **Drawer pull.** Touched every single time the box is opened. A ribbon pull
   costs almost nothing and changes how the box feels in the hand.
4. **Foil or emboss on the brand mark.** Small area, disproportionate effect.
5. **Magnetic closure.** Real cost, largely wasted on a drawer box where the
   drawers do the work.

## Inner containers

The drawer is presentation. These are the actual barrier.

**Saffron — glass vial or small tin.** Amber glass or an opaque tin. Light is
saffron's main enemy and a clear vial on a shop shelf degrades the product while
displaying it. If a clear vial is chosen for display reasons, the outer box must
stay closed in storage and that becomes a shelf instruction. Vial must be fitted
so it cannot move in transit — a loose vial rattling in a premium box is worse
than no vial.

**Herbs — sealed pouches or tins.** Foil-lined pouch is cheapest and seals best;
a tin looks better and is reusable, which matters more in a gift than it sounds.
Kakuti leaf and rose buds are display items and earn a window; kakuti powder and
rose petals do not need one.

**Tea bags — individually overwrapped.** Non-negotiable. Unwrapped bags scent the
entire box within weeks and the saffron picks it up first.

## The window tension

Windows show the product and let in the light that degrades it. Resolve per
drawer rather than as a blanket rule:

| Component | Window? | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Rose buds | Yes | The visual hero. Hiding them wastes the best-looking thing in the box. |
| Kakuti leaf | Yes | Unfamiliar product — seeing it does sales work. |
| Saffron | No | Most light-sensitive and most valuable. Show it when the drawer opens, not through a window. |
| Rose petals | No | Colour fades fastest; a browned petal window is worse than no window. |
| Kakuti powder | No | Nothing to see. |

## Shelf life

Whole box life is set by the shortest-lived component, and Gulf importers apply a
minimum-remaining-shelf-life rule at the border. Establish each component's real
life in its chosen container before declaring anything — the declared figure is
a commitment that ages against stock sitting in an Omani warehouse, not just
against the border.

## Print reality

- Order a **physical sample** before the run. Screen proofs mislead on colour,
  and they say nothing about how a drawer slides — which is the entire product
  experience.
- The palette hex values are screen values. Ask the vendor to match to a spot
  reference and check on the actual stock; saffron red on uncoated board is not
  saffron red on a monitor.
- Leave a surface designed to take the production and expiry date overprint. An
  overprint landing on a textured or foiled area smudges.
