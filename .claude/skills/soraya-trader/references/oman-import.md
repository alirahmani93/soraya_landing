# Importing food into Oman

Public research, August 2026. Regulations change; treat this as the briefing you
take to a licensed customs broker, not as the ruling itself. Confirm anything
that costs money before acting on it.

## Who may import

The importing entity must hold a valid **commercial import licence** issued by
the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (**MOCIIP**). Food
importers are additionally subject to health-authority oversight on registration
and clearance of food consignments.

For Soraya this means the Omani family company — or the Omani buyer — is the
importer of record. The Iranian farm cannot be.

## Documents to expect

- Commercial invoice, itemised, with HS codes and per-line net/gross weights
- Packing list matching the invoice line for line
- **Certificate of origin** — Iranian, chamber-attested
- **Phytosanitary certificate** — Iran's Plant Protection Organization, Ministry
  of Jihad-e-Agriculture. Attests the consignment meets phytosanitary import
  requirements for dried plant material.
- **Health certificate** for foodstuffs
- **Laboratory analysis** — commonly requested. An ISO 3632 saffron assay serves
  double duty as a marketing claim, so obtain it regardless.
- Bill of lading / air waybill
- Import licence details of the consignee

## Labelling — GSO requirements

Oman adopted the GCC standards **GSO 9** (labelling of prepackaged foodstuffs)
and **GSO 150** (shelf life), with compliance expected against the GCC standard
as currently in force.

Mandatory on the original label or primary packaging, **in Arabic**:

| Field | Note for Soraya |
|---|---|
| Product name | Both the SKU name and what it actually is |
| Brand name | ثريا |
| Country of origin | **Iran.** Not Oman. |
| Ingredients, descending by proportion | Per drawer and for the tea-bag blend |
| Additives | None expected — say so rather than omitting the line |
| Origin of animal fat | Not applicable; the product is entirely plant |
| Net content, metric | Grams. Forces the kakuti leaf count to resolve to a weight. |
| Production date | Requires an on-pack print or overprint step |
| Expiry date | Same |
| Manufacturer / importer / distributor name and address | Both sides of the Iran–Oman split |
| Storage and preparation instructions | Airtight, away from light; saffron degrades on both |

Arabic-only or Arabic + English. English-only may be approved by exception for
marketing purposes, but do not design around an exception. Arabic stickers over a
foreign label are accepted in practice and used routinely by exporters — they are
also visibly a patch, which is the wrong signal on a premium gift box.

## Shelf life

Oman enforces GCC shelf-life standards. Gulf importers, Oman among them,
generally require a **minimum remaining shelf life** on arrival — commonly 50% of
the declared total, with category-specific exemptions. For products outside the
prescribed list, the manufacturer's declared shelf life is accepted.

Practical reading: the shelf life you declare is not free. Declaring longer
raises the absolute amount of life that must remain at the border, and it ages
against stock sitting in the Omani warehouse too.

## Duty and tax

GCC operates a common external tariff; the applicable rate follows the HS heading,
which is exactly why the tea-bag classification question is worth settling early.
Oman VAT applies at the standard rate of 5%; confirm the Omani entity's
registration status and whether it recovers input VAT.

Get the broker to confirm rates against the specific headings before quoting DDP.
